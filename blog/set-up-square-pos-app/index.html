Setting up the Square POS app doesn’t have to be a headache. In the first few minutes you can have a fully functional system ready to take sales.

This guide walks you through every step—from downloading the app to customizing tax settings—so you can start selling right away.

1. Prerequisites and Account Creation

1. Prerequisites and Account Creation

Before installing the Square POS app, make sure you have the essential hardware, connectivity, and a valid Square business account. Skipping any of these steps can cause delays when you try to process your first sale.

  • Device requirements: Square runs on iOS (iPhone, iPad) and Android tablets/phones. The device should be ≥ 9.0 (iOS) or ≥ 8.0 (Android) and have at least 2 GB of RAM for smooth performance. A 10‑inch tablet is ideal for a full‑screen view of the sales screen, but a smartphone works for pop‑up stalls.
  • Internet connection: A stable Wi‑Fi or 4G/5G connection is needed for real‑time syncing of sales, inventory, and tax calculations. Aim for a minimum of 5 Mbps download/upload speed; slower connections may trigger offline mode.
  • Power source: Keep the device plugged in or use a reliable power bank. Square devices can run for 6‑8 hours on a full charge, but a constant power supply avoids unexpected shutdowns during busy periods.

Creating a Square business account

  1. Visit squareup.com and click “Sign Up”.
  2. Enter your email, create a password, and choose “Business” as the account type.
  3. Provide your legal business name, address, and phone number. This information will appear on receipts and tax reports.
  4. Select the country where you operate. Square automatically adjusts tax settings for the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia.
  5. Verify your identity by uploading a government‑issued ID and a bank account statement (required for payouts). The verification process typically completes within 24 hours.

After the account is approved, log in on your device, and you’ll be ready to download the Square POS app and continue with the setup wizard.

2. Downloading and Installing the App

2. Downloading and Installing the App

Getting the Square POS app onto your device is the first concrete step toward a functional checkout system. The process differs slightly between iOS and Android, but both stores enforce version control, so you can be sure you are installing the latest, secure build.

  1. iOS (iPhone / iPad)
    • Open the App Store on your device.
    • Tap the search icon and type Square POS.
    • Locate the official app published by Square, Inc. – the developer name appears directly under the app title.
    • Check the version number displayed on the app’s page. As of 2024 the current release is 22.3.1; newer versions will have a higher number.
    • Tap Get and authenticate with Face ID, Touch ID, or your Apple ID password.
    • Wait for the download and automatic installation to complete (typically 30‑60 seconds on a 4G/5G connection).
  2. Android (Phone / Tablet)
    • Launch the Google Play Store.
    • Enter Square POS in the search bar.
    • Select the app whose publisher is listed as Square, Inc.. Verify the icon matches Square’s green‑white logo.
    • Review the “What’s new” section for the version number; the latest stable build in 2024 is 22.3.1 (or higher).
    • Tap Install. If prompted, allow the store to access your device storage – Square needs this for receipt PDFs and inventory images.
    • Installation usually finishes within a minute on a typical 3 Mbps mobile network.

Version verification after installation

  • Open the Square POS app.
  • Navigate to Settings → About.
  • Confirm the displayed version matches the latest version noted on the store page.
  • If the numbers differ, return to the store and tap Update before proceeding.

Keeping the app up to date is essential because Square releases monthly patches that address security, payment‑processing latency, and new hardware compatibility – all critical for a small business that cannot afford downtime.

3. Initial Setup Wizard Walkthrough

3. Initial Setup Wizard Walkthrough

When you launch the Square POS app for the first time, the wizard walks you through four essential screens. I built similar flows for retail and restaurant clients in Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Pakistan, so I know how every toggle can either simplify or clutter a shop’s day‑to‑day.

  1. Business Info – Enter your shop name, legal entity type and contact email. For non‑restaurant shops I always turn off the “Restaurant Mode” switch; this removes menu‑related tabs that would otherwise appear later. The screen also asks if you want to enable “Online Ordering.” Keep it off unless you actually sell food delivery.
  2. Currency – Choose the primary currency (USD, SAR, AED, PKR, etc.). Square lets you add a secondary display currency; I recommend leaving it blank for small shops to avoid confusion on receipts.
  3. Location – Set your physical address and time zone. This data feeds tax calculations and receipt footers. If you operate multiple counters in the same shop, create separate locations later, but for a single‑counter store keep just one entry.
  4. Default Tax Rates – Square shows a list of common tax presets. Turn off any “VAT/ GST” options you don’t need; you can always add a custom rate later. For Saudi and UAE shops that must comply with ZATCA Phase 2, enable the “ZATCA e‑invoicing” toggle and set the 15 % tax rate. For Pakistani retailers, you may leave the tax toggle off if you’re not yet registered with FBR.

After the four screens, the wizard asks if you’d like to import items from a CSV. I skip this step for a clean start and add products manually later.

Note: The system supports bilingual Arabic/English invoices and a one‑time license that keeps the software running even if the license server goes down.

4. Configuring Taxes for Your Region

4. Configuring Taxes for Your Region

Accurate tax settings prevent revenue‑leakage and keep your reports compliant. Square lets you create tax tables for each jurisdiction you sell in, then apply them to items or entire sales.

  1. Open Tax Settings: From the Square Dashboard, go to Settings ▶ Taxes. Click + Add Tax.
  2. Select a tax type: Choose Sales Tax for U.S. states, VAT for the United Kingdom, GST/HST for Canada, or GST for Australia.
  3. Enter the rate: Input the percentage (e.g., 6.25 % for California). Use the Custom Rate field for special districts or reduced‑rate items.
  4. Assign to locations: If you operate in multiple states or provinces, tick the locations where the tax applies.
  5. Save and repeat for each jurisdiction you need.

Below is a quick reference for the most common rates. Verify current rates with local tax authorities before entering them.

For custom rates—such as a city tax of 1.5 % in New York City—click + Add Tax and select Custom Rate. Name the tax (e.g., “NYC City Tax”), set the percentage, and link it to the appropriate location.

Once taxes are defined, you can apply them automatically:

  • Enable Auto‑apply tax on the item level to have Square add the correct rate during checkout.
  • Use Tax overrides for exempt items (e.g., food‑service meals in some states).

Typical price ranges for tax configuration services range from $50 – $150 per jurisdiction when outsourcing to a developer.

5. Adding Items and Inventory Management

5. Adding Items and Inventory Management

Accurate item data is the backbone of a smooth checkout experience. Square POS lets you create a detailed catalog, assign SKUs, scan barcodes, and set up stock alerts—all from within the app or via a quick Excel import.

Step‑by‑step checklist
  1. Create categories – Navigate to Items → Categories. Group products by type (e.g., "Beverages," "Bakery," "Apparel"). This hierarchy appears on the sales screen, making it faster for staff to locate items.
  2. Add a single item – Tap + Add Item and fill in:
    • Name – clear, customer‑facing title.
    • SKU – a unique alphanumeric code (e.g., "BKG‑001").
    • Price – set the retail price; typical small‑shop margins range from $2.99 to $49.99.
    • Barcode – choose Generate or scan an existing UPC/EAN.
    • Category – assign from the list created earlier.
  3. Bulk import with Excel – If you already track inventory in a spreadsheet, export it as .csv with columns matching Square’s template (Name, SKU, Price, Category, Quantity, Barcode). In the Square Dashboard, go to Items → Import and upload the file. The import process validates data and instantly populates your catalog.
  4. Set stock alerts – Open an item, scroll to Inventory, and enable Low stock alert. Define a threshold (e.g., 10 units). Square will send an email or push notification when inventory falls below this level, helping you reorder before stockouts.
  5. Enable barcode scanning – Connect a Bluetooth barcode scanner to the device running Square POS. When scanning, the app automatically pulls up the matched item, reducing manual entry errors.

By following this workflow, you create a searchable, barcode‑ready catalog that scales as your product line grows. The Excel import method mirrors the simple approach described in the “Inventory‑in‑Excel” guide, allowing you to transition from a manual spreadsheet to a fully integrated POS with minimal disruption.

6. Setting Up Payments and Receipts

6. Setting Up Payments and Receipts

Getting the Square POS app to accept money and print or email receipts is the final piece that turns a device into a functional checkout station. Follow these steps to link a bank account, enable the desired payment methods, and tailor receipt templates to match your brand.

  1. Link a bank account
    • Open the Square app and tap Settings → Bank Accounts.
    • Select Add Bank Account. Enter your routing and account numbers; Square will verify the account with two small deposits (usually <$0.10) within 1‑3 business days.
    • Once verified, set the account as the Primary Deposit Destination so all sales settle automatically.
  2. Enable payment types
    • Navigate to Settings → Payments.
      1. Toggle Card Payments to accept Visa, Mastercard, Amex, and Discover.
      2. Turn on Contactless & Chip for tap‑to‑pay (Apple Pay, Google Pay, etc.).
      3. Activate Cash if you want the app to record cash transactions for reporting.
    • Set default transaction limits (e.g., $500 per card swipe) to reduce fraud risk.
  3. Customize receipt templates
    • Go to Settings → Receipts.
      1. Choose Email or Print as the default format.
      2. Upload a logo (PNG / JPG, max 500 KB) and enter your business name, address, and phone number.
      3. Toggle optional fields: Itemized list, Tax breakdown, Tip amount, and Custom message (e.g., “Thank you for supporting local business”).
    • Preview the receipt using the Sample Receipt button; adjust font size or line spacing if the layout looks cramped.
    • Save changes. All future transactions will use the new template automatically.

Typical processing fees for card payments range from 2.6% + $0.10 per swipe for in‑person transactions to 3.5% + $0.15 for online or keyed‑in cards. Cash payments incur no processing fee, making them a cost‑effective fallback for small purchases.

Checklist before you go live:

  • Bank account linked and verified.
  • Card, contactless, and cash options enabled.
  • Receipt template branded and tested.
  • Processing fee structure understood.

With payments and receipts configured, the Square POS app is ready to handle real sales, and you can move on to testing transactions and launching your store.

7. Offline Mode and Data Backup

7. Offline Mode and Data Backup

Square’s POS can keep ringing up sales even when the internet drops. In offline mode the app stores transactions locally and syncs them automatically once connectivity returns. From my own work building a full‑stack POS for Saudi and UAE shops, I learned that relying solely on cloud sync is risky – many owners prefer a tangible backup they can open on a phone or PC.

Why you should back up locally

  • Instant access: A daily CSV or Excel file can be opened without logging into Square.
  • Owner confidence: Shopkeepers I’ve worked with trust a WhatsApp file more than a web dashboard.
  • Regulatory safety: In Saudi Arabia the ZATCA Phase 2 e‑invoicing rules require you to retain records for at least 5 years; a local copy satisfies that requirement.

Step‑by‑step: Export daily sales report

  1. Open the Square Dashboard on your tablet or PC.
  2. Tap Reports → Sales Summary.
  3. Select the date range – usually “Yesterday” for a daily backup.
  4. Press the Export button and choose CSV or Excel.
  5. Save the file to a folder that syncs with your local drive (e.g., C:\SquareBackups).
  6. Open WhatsApp on your phone, start a chat with yourself or a trusted backup number.
  7. Attach the saved file and send it. Label it with the date for easy retrieval.

For shops that still run Windows 7, the same export works; Square’s web interface is browser‑based, so there’s no OS limitation.

Note: This system is sold as a full version with no feature tiers. The POS supports Saudi ZATCA Phase 2 e‑invoicing; invoices and bills for Saudi/UAE shops are printed in both Arabic and English.

8. Test Transaction and Go Live

8. Test Transaction and Go Live

Before customers rely on the new system, run a complete test sale to confirm that every component—hardware, software, tax settings, and payment processing—behaves as expected. Follow the checklist below, then switch the app to live mode.

  1. Perform a sandbox transaction
    • Open the Square POS app and select Settings → Developer (if available) to enable the sandbox environment.
    • Add a test item (e.g., "Test Coffee" priced at $3.99) to the catalog.
    • Use a test card number provided by Square (e.g., 4111 1111 1111 1111) or a physical card with a small amount (under $5) to complete the sale.
  2. Verify the receipt
    • Check that the printed or emailed receipt shows the correct item name, price, tax, and total.
    • Confirm that the receipt format matches your branding settings.
  3. Review the transaction report
    • Navigate to Reports → Sales Summary and locate the test sale.
    • Ensure the amount, tax rate, and payment method are recorded accurately.
  4. Check hardware integration
    • Confirm that the card reader, receipt printer, and cash drawer (if used) responded correctly.
    • Test barcode scanning if you rely on inventory scans.

Troubleshooting common errors

  • Card reader not connecting: Restart the device, ensure Bluetooth is enabled, and verify the reader’s firmware is up‑to‑date via the Square Dashboard.
  • Tax mismatch: Re‑visit Settings → Taxes and confirm the correct jurisdiction and rate (e.g., 7.25% sales tax for California). Re‑run the test sale after adjustments.
  • Receipt not printing: Check printer paper, connection status, and that the correct printer is selected in Settings → Hardware.
  • Transaction not appearing in reports: Refresh the dashboard, ensure the device has internet access, and verify you are viewing the correct date range.

Once the test sale passes all checks, switch the app to live mode:

  1. Go to Settings → Account and toggle Live Mode on.
  2. Perform a real, low‑value transaction (e.g., a $1.00 coffee) using an actual payment method to confirm end‑to‑end functionality.
  3. Monitor the first 24 hours of sales in the dashboard for any anomalies.

With the system verified, you are ready to accept real customer payments and focus on growing your business.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Square POS without an internet connection?

Yes. Square stores transactions locally for up to 72 hours and syncs when the device reconnects. This offline mode is essential for shops with unreliable internet.

Do I need a Square card reader to accept payments?

A card reader is optional for in‑person card payments, but you can still accept cash, contactless NFC, and online payments through the app.

How do I set up sales tax for multiple locations?

Create separate locations in Square, then assign the appropriate tax rate to each. The app will apply the correct tax automatically at checkout.

Is there a way to export my sales data for accounting?

Square provides CSV and Excel exports. You can also schedule daily email reports, which can be forwarded to Gmail or WhatsApp for backup.

What hardware works best with Square POS?

Any iOS or Android tablet/phone works, but for faster checkout consider a Square Stand or a compatible barcode scanner.
