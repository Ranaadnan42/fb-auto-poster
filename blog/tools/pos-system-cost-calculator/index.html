Estimate what a point-of-sale system will really cost your business - hardware, software and card processing fees - and see whether a monthly subscription or a one-time license is cheaper over time.

How this POS cost calculator works

A point-of-sale system has three costs that many sellers quote separately: the hardware, the software and the card processing fees. The calculator adds all three so you can see the real total cost of ownership, not just the monthly price on a pricing page.

  • Hardware is a one-time purchase per checkout: a touch-screen terminal or PC, receipt printer, cash drawer, card reader, and for some businesses a barcode scanner or kitchen printer.
  • Software is either a monthly subscription per terminal (most cloud POS brands) or a one-time license with optional yearly support.
  • Card processing is a percentage of your card sales, usually between 2.3% and 3.5% depending on the provider and card type.

Subscription vs one-time license: which is cheaper?

Subscriptions cost little to start and include updates and cloud backups, which suits new businesses and multi-location chains. A one-time license costs more on day one but often becomes cheaper after 18-30 months, which is why many shop owners in markets like Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Pakistan prefer it. Change the "compare over" years in the tool to see where the two lines cross for your setup.

Hidden costs to ask every POS vendor about

  • Is card processing locked to their own payment provider?
  • Are there extra fees for inventory, loyalty, online ordering or extra users?
  • What happens to your sales data if you cancel?
  • Does the software keep working when the internet goes down?
  • Does it support your country's e-invoicing or tax rules (for example ZATCA in Saudi Arabia)?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a POS system cost for a small business?

For one checkout, expect roughly $800-$2,500 for hardware, plus either about $50-$165 per month for subscription software or a one-time license of roughly $300-$1,800. Card processing fees come on top and depend on your sales.

Is a cloud POS better than a one-time license POS?

Neither is always better. Cloud POS is cheaper to start and easy to manage across locations; a one-time license is often cheaper over three or more years and does not depend on a monthly payment. Compare total cost and offline support.

Can I use my existing computer or tablet as a POS?

Often yes. Many POS apps run on a normal Windows PC, iPad or Android tablet, which can save $500 or more per terminal. Untick the terminal option in the calculator to see the difference.

Are these POS prices exact?

No. They are typical market ranges to help you budget and compare quotes. Prices vary by brand, country, contract length and promotions, so always confirm with the vendor.
